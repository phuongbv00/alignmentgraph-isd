\begin{abstract}
Current commercial LLMs already provide strong support for instructional systems
design. However, dependence on third-party APIs, high costs, and data
safety risks remain barriers to practical deployment in educational institutions.
Open-weight SLMs can be deployed on-premise at reasonable cost and therefore offer
a more viable path, but as models become smaller, output quality depends
increasingly on the surrounding agent harness. Research on ISD agents has not yet
treated quality retention under model downscaling as a central problem, despite
its practical importance. Grounded in constructive alignment theory, we therefore
propose AlignmentGraph-ISD, a multi-agent harness that organizes the ISD process
around an alignment graph, that is, an explicit alignment-state graph. We evaluate
it with ISD-Agent-Bench and a deterministic, no-LLM alignment-signal measurement
across a four-size Qwen model ladder (0.8B--9B). As models shrink, the harness remains competitive and
robust, with a 0\% failure rate, satisfies the panel advantage
rule on measurable alignment signals ($\geq$4 of 6 directional signals are
significantly positive at every size), and leads on ADDIE score at the three
smaller sizes (\rqOneHarnessAddieTwoB{} versus \rqOneBestBaselineAddieTwoB{} for
the strongest baseline at 2B). Its gap widens significantly toward smaller models
before reaching parity with the strongest baseline at 9B. This robustness comes
with significantly lower Trajectory scores than the leading single-loop systems
and token costs above the baseline median. Beyond quantitative measurement, the harness's explicit
alignment graph lets instructional designers audit and refine final outputs,
supporting teacher-in-the-loop practice in contrast to automated pipelines with
limited auditability.
\end{abstract}
