\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Three lines of work meet in this study: agent systems for instructional design,
small and on-premise language models in education, and the measurement of
constructive alignment.

\subsection{LLM and Agent Systems for Instructional Design}

ISD-Agent-Bench collects theory-grounded agents implementing iterative tool use,
ADDIE, systematic design, and rapid prototyping~\cite{isdagentbench}; its ReAct
variant draws on reasoning--acting orchestration~\cite{yao2023react}. The benchmark
scores both artifacts and trajectories with LLM rubrics, but does not separately
instrument relations among generated objectives, activities, and assessments.
Related systems support pedagogy-guided authoring, direct plan generation,
multi-agent revision, and multi-agent course generation over commercial APIs
~\cite{lessonplanner2024,hu2024teachingplan,zhang2025eduplanner,
yao2025instructionalagents}. Across this work, preserving quality while reducing
the foundation model is not a joint harness-design and evaluation target.

\subsection{Small and On-Premise Language Models in Education}

A second line examines why and how institutions run language models locally.
Financial constraints slow AI adoption in higher education~\cite{robert2025educause},
learner-data privacy under third-party APIs remains an open ethical concern
~\cite{yan2024ethical}, and universities have built their own gateways to control
budgets and data~\cite{mithun2025aiverde}; self-hosting open-weight models is also
cheaper than APIs at sufficient volume~\cite{pan2025onpremise}. Concrete educational
deployments follow: open LLM infrastructure for a distance university
~\cite{zesch2024flexi}, evaluations of self-hosted models for schools
~\cite{chan2026bigger}, and locally run content authoring for privacy and cost
~\cite{reza2025smallmodels}. On the capability side, SLMs are argued to suffice for
much agentic workload when the architecture carries the coordination
~\cite{belcak2025slm,sharma2025slmagents}, agent scaffolding retains most quality
at the 0.5B--3B scale~\cite{kang2025agentdistill}, and decomposed multi-agent
framing narrows the gap between small and large models
~\cite{shen2024multillmagent}---although harness sensitivity is not monotonic
across model tiers~\cite{cho2026harness}. Closest to ISD, recent studies touch one
part of the problem each~\cite{jaldi2026slmassessment,zheng2025lessonplanlm,
lee2026educlaw,wei2026slmeducation}; none evaluates an end-to-end ISD agent
harness across a controlled range of model sizes.

\subsection{Constructive Alignment and Its Measurement}

Constructive alignment locates instructional quality in the relation among intended
learning outcomes, teaching activities, and assessment rather than in any artifact
alone~\cite{biggs1996enhancing,biggs2011teaching}. Quality Matters makes these
relations reviewable by requiring measurable objectives and supporting assessments,
materials, and activities~\cite{qm2018rubric}. Porter measures agreement between
content--demand distributions, Fulmer examines interpretation of that index, and
Webb evaluates facets including cognitive-demand consistency
~\cite{porter2002measuring,fulmer2011estimating,webb1997criteria}. The revised
Bloom taxonomy supplies a shared demand vocabulary~\cite{anderson2001taxonomy}.
These foundations motivate several complementary signals, but concern curricula,
standards, and assessments authored before measurement.

Automated measurement supplies relevant components. Semantic traceability and
embeddings recover relations among textual artifacts
~\cite{clelandhuang2018traceability,feng2022labse}; other work maps assessment
items to standards fixed before scoring~\cite{xu2025itemalignment}. For cognitive
demand, Li et al. provide the expert-labeled corpus used to fine-tune our Bloom
classifier~\cite{li2022bloomclassification}, while BloomNet is a related classifier
rather than the corpus source~\cite{waheed2021bloomnet}. These methods avoid a
generative judge, but do not jointly assess artifacts emitted by the system being
measured.
