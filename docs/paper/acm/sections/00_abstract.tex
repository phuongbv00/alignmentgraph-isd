\begin{abstract}
Agent systems built on commercial large language models (LLMs) already support
much of instructional systems design (ISD), but their dependence on third-party
APIs---inference cost, and learner data leaving the institution---is a documented
barrier to institutional deployment. Open-weight small language
models (SLMs) running on-premise are the practical alternative, and they shift the quality
burden from the model to the system around it. What fails first is constructive
alignment---the coherence among objectives, activities and assessments---which
existing systems leave the model to maintain implicitly in prose. Research on ISD agents has not treated quality retention under downscaling as a
design and evaluation goal, nor offered a way to observe alignment that does not
rest on a judge model. We propose
AlignmentGraph-ISD, a multi-agent system (MAS) that organizes ADDIE generation
around an alignment graph: explicit, checkable alignment state, exported after
every run for audit. We evaluate it with ISD-Agent-Bench and a deterministic,
no-LLM alignment-score measurement over 90 scenarios, six comparison systems and a
four-size Qwen ladder (0.8B--9B), plus a factorial ablation crossing decomposition
against substrate. Results on robustness, ADDIE quality, alignment
scores, and the cost trade-off are \TBD. The exported graph lets instructional designers audit and revise
the relations behind a design, supporting teacher-in-the-loop practice.
\end{abstract}
