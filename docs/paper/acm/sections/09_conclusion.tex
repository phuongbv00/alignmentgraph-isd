\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

As organizations generate instructional programs at scale and move to cost-efficient
small language models, the quality that matters most---constructive alignment among
objectives, activities, and assessments---is the first property to break, and it
breaks invisibly. Existing ISD agents only encourage alignment through prompting,
leaving quality dependent on raw model capability. We presented
AlignmentGraph-ISD, an agent harness that reifies constructive alignment as an
explicit, verifiable alignment graph and organizes generation around it: specialist
agents with bounded write scope build the graph so alignment holds by construction,
and each specialist verifies and repairs its pedagogical constraints as a tool
within its own turn. On ISD-Agent-Bench, the harness achieves the highest overall
score among seven agents under both a large model and a 9B SLM, and an ablation
shows that multi-agent decomposition over the graph is the dominant contributor,
whose effect roughly doubles as the model shrinks (from $+3.9$ to $+7.9$ composite
points, $p<10^{-12}$), while verification/repair acts as a guarantee mechanism whose
effect is visible in a residual-defect audit rather than in the judged score. Representing alignment as
first-class graph state, and decomposing generation around it, is thus what lets a
harness preserve instructional-design quality as a property of the system rather
than of the model. Extending the harness to multi-session refinement, and closing
the evaluation construct gap so pedagogical rigor is credited fairly, are the
natural next steps.
