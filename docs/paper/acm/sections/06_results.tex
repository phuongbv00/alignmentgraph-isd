\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Tables and figures in this section are generated from artifacts; every mean is
paired with the between-run SD of the same score, and cells with fewer than
\ladderNumScenarios{} scenarios are marked in footnotes. Because every score
comparison is conditional on an agent producing output at all, we present
output robustness first.

\subsection{Output robustness by model size}
\label{sec:robustness}

AlignmentGraph-ISD produced usable output for every scenario at every size,
whereas the two least scaffolded agents lost most of their outputs at the small
end (\Cref{fig:failure-rate}); the failure-rate differences are significant
under Fisher tests with Holm correction. The failure mechanisms differ: for
\costWorstCoverageAgent{} at \costWorstCoverageSize{},
\costWorstCoverageTruncPct\% of missing artifacts ended in
\texttt{LengthFinishReasonError} at exactly
\texttt{completion\_tokens = 8192}, whereas EduPlanner failures were dominated
by schema parsing. Under the envelope in \Cref{sec:assumptions}, this is a
deployability result: one-shot generation often cannot finish a design within
the call budget of the deployment target, while individual specialist calls
stay far below the limit. Because truncation does not explain every failure,
score analyses use \texttt{failure=0} as the primary layer and complete-case
as sensitivity.

\subsection{Objective 2: Quality on LLM judge scores}
\label{sec:rq1}

On ADDIE, AlignmentGraph-ISD leads at the three smaller sizes and reaches
parity with ReAct-ISD at 9B (\Cref{tab:rq1-ladder}, \Cref{fig:rq1-components}).
ADDIE leads this account because its rubric matches the construct Objective~2
addresses, not because it was chosen as an endpoint; the three LLM judge scores have
equal standing, and their disagreements are stated directly below. Full
per-score tables for Trajectory and \texttt{total\_score} appear in
\Cref{sec:appendix-tables}.

The proposed system produces stronger ADDIE artifacts, but its multi-agent
architecture with a verify--repair loop yields a longer, less compact trace
than single-loop designs, so Trajectory favors ReAct-ISD and ADDIE-Agent at
every size (\Cref{fig:rq1-components}, middle panel), and the
\texttt{total\_score} composite inherits this process penalty: at the smallest
size, the MAS reaches \rqOneHarnessTotalZeroEightB{} versus
\rqOneBestBaselineTotalZeroEightB{} for \rqOneBestBaselineNameZeroEightB{},
despite leading ADDIE and the alignment panel. The ADDIE gap is also uneven: at
0.8B, the delta against the strongest baseline
(\rqOneBestBaselineNameZeroEightB{}) is $\rqOneDeltaBestAddieZeroEightB$ and
not significant after Holm ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}$ at most
$\rqOnePHolmMaxZeroEightB$), while at 4B the delta against ReAct-ISD narrows
to $\rqOneDeltaBestAddieFourB$. At the large end, the ADDIE difference from
ReAct-ISD is $\rqOneDeltaBestAddieNineB$ with bootstrap 95\% CI
$[\rqOneDeltaBestAddieCILoNineB,\rqOneDeltaBestAddieCIHiNineB]$; this supports
a conclusion of parity, not superiority.

The scale interaction, by contrast, is clear: against \rqOneDiDBaselineName{},
the largest-minus-smallest ADDIE DiD is $\rqOneDiDVsBestBaseline$ (95\% CI
$[\rqOneDiDVsBestBaselineCILo,\rqOneDiDVsBestBaselineCIHi]$,
$p=\rqOneDiDVsBestBaselineP$). In the primary layer, the MAS's advantage
contracts as the model grows on all three LLM judge scores. Failures concentrate
at the small end, so the very large deltas against Baseline and EduPlanner at
0.8B stand on very small complete-case scenario counts (column $n$,
\Cref{tab:rq1-ladder}): they are artifacts of failure rate and cannot be read
as quality gaps. This is precisely the selection effect that the
\texttt{failure=0} layer avoids.

\subsection{Objective 3: Quality on alignment scores}
\label{sec:rq2}

Under the pre-specified interpretation rule, the panel concludes an advantage
for AlignmentGraph-ISD at every size: at least four of the six directional
scores are significantly positive after within-score Holm correction, and no
score is significantly negative (\Cref{tab:rq2-panel}; the full 7-score
$\times$ 7-agent picture is in \Cref{fig:rq2-components}). Family A separates
most clearly on $\sigma_{\mathrm{meas}}$, the score corresponding to the
\emph{measures} facet of \Cref{eq:aligned}; the Bloom-based Family B
contributes independent evidence through congruence, Porter, and Webb. The
reverse-direction score $\sigma_{\mathrm{rev}}$ remains diagnostic and does
not participate in the directional count.

The graph-context mechanism carries objectives into each specialist's
generation call; this explains the broad textual-correspondence profile, while
Family B checks cognitive demand through an entirely different mechanism, so
the agreement of the two families supports multiple measurable signals rather
than one composite claim. Against ReAct-ISD, the strongest comparator, the
per-score deltas are significantly positive at every size
(\Cref{fig:rq2-forest}), and the $\sigma_{\mathrm{meas}}$ gap widens toward
smaller models: the DiD is $\rqTwoSimDiDVsBaseline$ (95\% CI
$[\rqTwoSimDiDVsBaselineCILo,\rqTwoSimDiDVsBaselineCIHi]$,
$p=\rqTwoSimDiDVsBaselineP$). $\beta_{\mathrm{webb}}$ is the adverse exception
at small models; the profile must therefore be read score by score.

\label{sec:sensitivity}
This reading does not depend on the instrument configuration. The direction of
the pairwise comparisons favoring AlignmentGraph-ISD persists across
independent neural-encoder lineages, the TF--IDF lexical floor, the
BERT--lexicon Bloom axis, and the complete-case slice: on the encoder axis the
system remains first in \rqTwoSensTopOneCellsEncoderAxis{} arm--size cells,
and on the Bloom axis in \rqTwoSensTopOneCellsBloomAxis{} cells. The
comparative conclusion therefore does not depend on one specific encoder, on
semantic embeddings as a method class, or on one specific Bloom classifier
(\Cref{tab:rq2-sensitivity}). The complete-case result is sensitivity
evidence, not an unconditional analysis.

\subsection{Ablation study: Mechanism evidence}
\label{sec:ablation}

The ablation results are judge-invariant but alignment-shifted: removing graph
context changes ADDIE scores very little (\Cref{tab:ablation}) but lowers the
alignment panel broadly (\Cref{fig:ablation-components}); removing the verifier from
the shipped configuration produces no significant score effect; and every arm
produces complete output. The mechanism picture thus separates into three
layers. The decomposition layer (kept by every arm, not ablated) is associated
with what persists across all arms---robustness and the ADDIE advantage over
baselines: that advantage remains intact even with graph context disabled, so
the ablation does not explain the gap on LLM judge scores, and without a monolithic control no
causal credit can be assigned to decomposition. The graph-context layer is the
mechanism supported by the evidence for the alignment scores. The verifier
layer produces no measurable improvement in the shipped configuration and at
most sizes lowers Trajectory; its role is a repair backstop and a source of
audit evidence. When graph context is disabled, the verifier's simple effect
on $\sigma_{\mathrm{meas}}$ is positive at every size, from
\ablVerifCtxOffNineB{} to \ablVerifCtxOffZeroEightB{}; this counterfactual
does not establish a score effect in the shipped system.

\subsection{Cost and throughput}
\label{sec:cost}

The MAS uses \costHarnessCallsTwoB{} calls and \costHarnessTokensTwoB{}
thousand tokens per design at 2B; its token use is
\costTokenRatioVsMedianBaselineNineB$\times$--
\costTokenRatioVsMedianBaselineZeroEightB$\times$ the median baseline and
\costTokenRatioVsCheapestCompleteZeroEightB$\times$--
\costTokenRatioVsCheapestCompleteTwoB$\times$ ReAct-ISD, the cheapest baseline
with complete coverage. Tokens per ADDIE point favor the trade-off at the two
smaller sizes, deteriorate at 4B, and lose at 9B, where ReAct-ISD reaches
parity or better with approximately
$1/\costTokenRatioVsCheapestCompleteNineB$ of the tokens
(\Cref{fig:cost-quality}). Component telemetry attributes
\costVerifierTokenOverheadFourB\%--\costVerifierTokenOverheadTwoB\% token
overhead to the verifier and \costGraphContextTokenOverheadZeroEightB\%--
\costGraphContextTokenOverheadTwoB\% to graph context; graph context creates
the larger overhead and is also the mechanism supported by the alignment-panel ablation.
