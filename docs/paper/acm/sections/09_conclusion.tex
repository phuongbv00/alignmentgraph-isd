\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Constructive alignment is hard to inspect when objectives, activities, assessments
and delivery are generated as one fluent document, and harder still under a
small-model serving envelope, where the system must preserve those relations without
placing the whole task in a single call.

This paper contributes AlignmentGraph-ISD, a multi-agent system that runs the six
ADDIE concerns as separate stages over a shared alignment graph, commits every
cross-artifact relation as typed state at the moment it is created, checks that state
deterministically, and exports it after every run so that a design can be audited
rather than only read. The division of labour is explicit: the model writes
instructional content, and the harness decides what is asked for, what is committed,
what is checked, and what is reported. Where a relation is missing, the system says
so instead of filling it in.

Every system is scored both by the benchmark's LLM judges and by a no-LLM panel of
six alignment signals covering textual correspondence and cognitive demand. That panel
measures signals of constructive alignment; it does not validate the construct, and a
small blinded expert review accompanies it for that reason.

Results from the ladder comparison and the factorial ablation are \TBD{}, and the
claims this paper makes will be stated at the level each experiment supports: system
behaviour across the ladder from the first, attribution between decomposition and
substrate from the second, and no transfer between them. Conclusions will hold on the
Qwen3.5 0.8B--9B ladder and on this benchmark, and the additional cost of explicit
alignment state will be reported as the trade it is.
