\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:experimental-methodology}

\subsection{Objectives and analyses}
\label{sec:rqs}

Objective~1 is met by the MAS in \Cref{sec:method}; the remaining three map onto
two experiments. Experiment~1 (TN1) is the ladder comparison against the benchmark
agents and answers Objectives~2 and~3. Experiment~2 (TN2) is the factorial ablation
and answers Objective~4. Objective~2 has two parts---the level at each size and the
scale interaction---which have equal standing and are tested by two separate
analyses (per-size and difference-in-differences, \Cref{sec:stats}); significance at
one size must not be read as evidence about downscaling. Objective~3 applies the
same two analyses to the alignment panel and stops at measurable signals; validating
the construct requires expert evidence beyond automated scores.

Both experiments run on a configuration frozen before either begins: code and
prompt hashes, model checkpoints, dataset splits, seeds, decoding and serving
settings, output and trajectory caps, LLM judge prompts, metric versions, and the panel
interpretation rule. Nothing in that list is revisited after results are seen, and a
cell is re-run only for a genuine technical failure.

\subsection{Comparison conditions}
\label{sec:conditions}

TN1 compares seven systems: AlignmentGraph-ISD and six benchmark agents (Baseline,
EduPlanner, ADDIE-Agent, RPISD-Agent, Dick-Carey-Agent, and ReAct-ISD), at every
size.

TN2 is a $2\times2$ factorial over the two design choices of \Cref{sec:method}
(\Cref{tab:ablation-arms}). The first factor is \emph{decomposition}: \texttt{multi}
runs the six stages as six agents, each starting in a fresh context under its own
identity, while \texttt{single} carries one context across all six stages under one
identity. The second factor is the \emph{substrate}: \texttt{graph} is the alignment
graph of \Cref{sec:alignment-graph}, while \texttt{prose} replaces it with a
graph-free blackboard in which each step deposits its parsed output under a document
key and later steps read whole documents back verbatim. The proposed system is the
\texttt{multi}$\times$\texttt{graph} cell.

Each factor is a bundle, and saying so is part of the claim rather than a caveat
attached to it. Decomposition varies the context boundary, how many personas the
run has, and whether work is delegated---these travel together because they are what
``multi-agent'' means to a reader, and separating them would test something no one
builds. The substrate factor likewise removes the whole alignment machinery: without
the graph there is no cross-artifact relation, no coverage audit, no reconciliation
pass, and no graph dump, so only the within-artifact checks survive.

The arms are not held to equal model effort, and equal effort is not the parity
condition. An agent that plans its own order and writes its own batches cannot spend
the same number of calls as a harness walking a fixed plan with developer agents;
that difference \emph{is} the treatment. The condition that must hold, and that we
verify, is that no arm was cut short by its turn budget---while that holds, an
outcome difference cannot be attributed to the ceiling.

We register one directional prediction before running TN2: under \texttt{single},
quality degrades with the position of a stage in the ADDIE sequence, and this
degradation is absent or weaker under \texttt{multi} and widens as the model
shrinks. TN2 is read out on the internal audit metrics and the alignment panel; the
LLM judge serves only as a cross-check and never leads the ablation narrative.

\subsection{Data, model ladder, and run structure}
\label{sec:dataset}
\label{sec:setup}

TN1 runs on the \texttt{test\_90} split of ISD-Agent-Bench~\cite{isdagentbench}: 90
scenarios drawn by stratified sampling with seed 42 and shared by all systems. TN2
runs on \texttt{test\_30}, a stratified 30-scenario split drawn with the same seed
and verified disjoint from the tuning split. No tuning or debugging output enters
either experiment; parameters were fixed on a separate training split before the
freeze.

The ladder comprises Qwen3.5 checkpoints at 0.8B, 2B, 4B, and 9B; holding the model
family constant means only capacity changes between sizes. TN1 covers all four
sizes, TN2 the two ends of the deployment envelope, 0.8B and 4B.

Each cell is generated once, at each agent's own sampling temperature, rather than
averaged over repeated runs. Uncertainty is carried by the paired, per-scenario analyses of
\Cref{sec:stats}, which resample scenarios rather than runs; with 90 paired
scenarios, scenario-level variation dominates the run-to-run variation that
repetition would estimate, and the saving is spent on the ablation and the expert
review instead. Repetition is used where it answers a question of its own: a
targeted repeat re-runs a small set of decisive cells---the proposed system, the
strongest baseline, and ReAct-ISD at 0.8B and 4B---and is reported as its own
robustness sub-study with its own $n$, not folded into the main estimates.

Scenarios and prompts for all seven systems are English by design. The only
baseline-specific intervention translates ReAct-ISD's prompt scaffolding into
English without altering its method.

\subsection{Deployment envelope and serving conditions}
\label{sec:assumptions}

The deployment target is a single 12--16\,GB GPU. At two bytes per parameter, bf16
weights require approximately 1.6\,GB for 0.8B, 4\,GB for 2B, 8\,GB for 4B, and
18\,GB for 9B; the 9B model therefore does not fit, and 4B is the largest in-target
checkpoint. The remaining VRAM must hold the KV cache and runtime overhead, leaving
a budget of roughly 8k tokens per call. This chain of reasoning is checkable
independently of experiment timing; 9B is an out-of-target reference point, not a
proposed deployment configuration.

All agents run through vLLM with streaming enabled and a common
generation cap. We cap generation at 8192 tokens per call---the budget a 4B model
leaves on a single 12--16\,GB GPU, the deployment target this work claims. The
24--32\,GB pods were rental convenience for running the ladder in parallel, not the
claimed envelope. Whether an architecture completes within this limit is therefore a
measured deployment property.

The proposed system plans every design at full scale---each assessment instrument
states how many items it has---and writes out a representative sample of it: a few
items per instrument, a short sequence of activities per module. The output states
the counts for each instrument. All arms of both experiments run under this
setting.

\subsection{Measures}
\label{sec:metrics}

Two measurement families are applied to the same saved outputs: the benchmark's
LLM judge scores, and a deterministic alignment-signal measurement that uses no LLM.
Their mechanisms differ, so agreement between them carries more weight than either
alone.

\subsubsection{LLM judge scores}

For Objective~2, one fixed LLM judge from a family separate from the agents scores every
output; this separation reduces self-preference risk~\cite{panickssery2024llm}. A
second, independently chosen LLM judge scores a stratified subset covering the proposed
system, ReAct-ISD, and the strongest baseline, to test whether the direction of the
conclusion depends on the LLM judge. The three LLM judge scores are reported in parallel
with identical analyses and equal standing (\Cref{tab:llm-judge-scores}); ADDIE leads
the narrative because its rubric matches the construct Objective~2 addresses, and
the composite is retained only for comparability with how ISD-Agent-Bench reports.

The trajectory score requires a disclosure that shapes how it may be read. The
benchmark shows the LLM judge only the first 20 tool calls of a run. A single-loop system
makes roughly five calls and is seen in full; a decomposed system makes one to two
orders of magnitude more and is seen only at the beginning, so a low trajectory score
may reflect a display limit rather than behaviour. We fix the policy before running
and report two layers: a benchmark-comparable layer at the original cap of 20, and a
diagnostic layer at a cap of 120, which covers a typical decomposed run end to end.
Full trajectories are stored, so the second layer is a re-scoring of the same
outputs and requires no regeneration. Neither cap is revisited after results are
seen.

\subsubsection{Alignment signals}
\label{sec:alignment-measures}

The LLM judge scores inherit the documented biases of rubric-scoring
LLMs~\cite{zheng2023judging,panickssery2024llm,norman2026reliability}, and they do
not expose alignment relations at all. The study therefore adds a second measurement
that reads the semantic layer corresponding to the structural relations the graph
makes explicit, on the rendered output of every system. It operationalizes
established criteria and follows two principles:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{No LLM in measurement}: there is no prompting, sampling, or matching
  threshold; once the encoder and Bloom classifier are pinned, every signal is a
  fixed function of text.
  \item \emph{Read only text from saved output}: self-declared graph identifiers are
  excluded from signal computation and retained only as validity evidence, so every
  system is measured on the same basis.
\end{itemize}

The panel is a pre-specified flat set of six alignment signals in two instrument
families. \emph{Family A} measures textual correspondence between artifacts with
embeddings; \emph{Family B} measures cognitive demand through Bloom levels. From
each output the measurement extracts three text sets: $\mathcal{O}$ (objectives),
$\mathcal{A}$ (assessment tasks), and $\mathcal{X}$ (learning activities). Family A
uses the mean--max rectified-cosine operator
\[
  \mathrm{sim}(S,T)
  = |S|^{-1}\sum_{u\in S}\;\max_{t\in T}\;\max\bigl(0,\cos(e_u,e_t)\bigr),
\]
where $e_u$ is the embedding of text span $u$; $\max(0,\cdot)$ is a scale choice on
$[0,1]$, not a matching threshold. \Cref{tab:panel-signals} specifies the six signals.
Family A is exactly the three edges of the constructive-alignment triad, one per core
relation of \Cref{eq:aligned}, so no panel signal measures something the graph does
not model.

The two families are independent in mechanism but not perfectly so, and the
exception belongs here rather than in a reviewer's report.
$\beta_{\mathrm{cong}}$ selects the assessment task to compare against by cosine
similarity before comparing Bloom levels, so it shares the embedding step with
Family~A. $\beta_{\mathrm{port}}$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{webb}}$ involve no similarity
at all and are therefore the full counterweights; agreement between Family~A and
those two carries the triangulation argument.

Both instruments are version-pinned. The encoder maps each text span to $e_u$; the
primary configuration uses Nemotron-8B, with alternative encoder arms in
\Cref{tab:rq2-arms}. The Bloom classifier assigns an Anderson--Krathwohl level to
each objective and assessment task; the primary configuration is a BERT checkpoint
fine-tuned on an expert-labeled learning-objective
corpus~\cite{li2022bloomclassification}, with a verb lexicon as the comparison arm.
The checkpoint always returns an argmax whereas the lexicon may abstain, so Family-B
denominators differ between the two Bloom arms. Sensitivity arms form a star around
the primary configuration---each deviates on exactly one axis, not a grid---and are
run on a subset rather than over the whole ladder.

The panel is reported flat, with no primary endpoint, for two reasons: constructive
alignment has several facets, and any composite would impose weights with no
theoretical basis. Each signal is named after the operation it computes, and none is
called ``precision''; \emph{alignment signal} is the collective term for the panel
values, while the construct itself lives in \Cref{eq:aligned}. The interpretation
rule is pre-specified: a size shows an alignment advantage over a comparison system
when at least four of the six signals are significantly positive after within-signal
Holm correction and none is significantly negative; the reversed pattern indicates a
disadvantage, and every other pattern---including five positive with one
significantly negative---is reported as mixed, with the losing side named. This is
not a composite: nothing is summed or normalized, and each signal keeps its own $p$.
We therefore claim only measurable signals of constructive alignment; the
measurement does not validate the construct, and that bridge needs the expert layer
below. Scoring runs offline on saved outputs, uses no LLM, and has no stochastic
component; results are reproducible given pinned encoder revisions and the embedding
cache, up to floating-point noise. Cost telemetry reads only
\texttt{metadata.token\_usage}.

\subsubsection{Expert review}
\label{sec:expert}

Automated signals alone cannot connect the panel to educational value, so a small
expert layer accompanies them. Three to five instructors experienced in course
design each review 8--12 outputs with the system name withheld, judging
constructive alignment, feasibility, clarity, and how much revision the design would
need before use. The review is conducted after the configuration freeze and on
official output, and is reported descriptively alongside the panel. It is not used
to select methods, adjust prompts, or choose among configurations; doing so after
seeing results would make the whole evaluation retrospective.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}
\label{sec:stats}

The unit of inference is the scenario. For the level part of Objective~2, paired
Wilcoxon signed-rank tests compare the proposed system with each baseline at every
size and on all three LLM judge scores; Objective~3 applies the same test to each
alignment signal. When a paired sample has $n\leq25$, we use the exact permutation
null. We report paired mean differences, rank-biserial effect sizes, and bootstrap
95\% confidence intervals from 10{,}000 scenario resamples with seed 42.

Holm correction covers the six proposed-versus-baseline comparisons per size,
separately for each LLM judge score and each alignment signal. Objective~3
additionally reports a panel-wide Holm correction over six signals and six
comparisons as a multiplicity sensitivity; that correction creates no hierarchy
among signals. For the scale part of Objective~2, let
$\Delta_s$ be the paired proposed-minus-baseline gap at size $s$. The pre-specified
interaction is $\Delta_{\mathrm{largest}}-\Delta_{\mathrm{smallest}}$, tested with
paired Wilcoxon and a bootstrap interval; a negative value means a larger advantage
at the small end. A difference observed at a single size is never read as a trend
across sizes. Objective~3 uses the same interaction, with $\sigma_{\mathrm{meas}}$
leading the scale narrative and the other signals reported alongside it.

For missing outputs, the primary layer assigns \texttt{failure=0}: a system that
produces no output has quality zero, not missing data. Complete-case inference is a
sensitivity analysis only, computed over available outputs and never described as
unconditional, because it conditions on a post-treatment variable---it removes
precisely the most severe failures, and does so most at the small sizes where the
hypothesis lives. Objective~2 additionally reports \texttt{failure=min};
Objective~3 does not, because its signals already floor at zero. Output-failure rates
are compared separately with Fisher's exact test under Holm correction.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:reproducibility}

Every measured value is generated from saved run, LLM judge, alignment, and ablation
artifacts and enters the paper only through generated files; no number is hand-typed.
The frozen manifest pins code and prompt hashes, checkpoints, splits, seeds,
decoding and serving settings, caps, LLM judge prompts, and metric versions, and
revision-aware embedding caches support exact re-analysis from saved vectors.
Because full trajectories and all outputs are stored, every reported statistic can
be recomputed without regenerating a single model response. Results produced before
the architecture pivot are excluded.
