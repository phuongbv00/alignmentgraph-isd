\begin{abstract}
Commercial LLMs already support instructional systems design well. However,
third-party API dependence, high cost, and learner-data safety risks hinder
real-world institutional deployment. Open-weight SLMs, deployable on-premise at
reasonable cost, offer a viable path --- but as models shrink, output quality
increasingly depends on the agent harness around them. ISD-agent research has not
treated quality retention under model downscaling as a central problem, despite its
practical significance. We therefore propose AlignmentGraph-ISD, grounded in
constructive alignment theory: a multi-agent harness that organizes the ISD process
around an explicit alignment state graph. Our evaluation combines ISD-Agent-Bench
with a self-developed alignment-measurement protocol across a ladder of four Qwen
model sizes (0.8B--9B). As models shrink, the harness stays robust with 0\%
failures, leads on the alignment panel at every size, and leads the ADDIE score
at the three smaller sizes (0.8B--4B) --- the gap widening toward smaller models ---
before converging with the strongest baseline at 9B. In return, Trajectory is
markedly lower than the leading single-loop systems, and token cost exceeds the
baseline median. Beyond these quantitative results, the explicit
alignment graph lets instructional designers audit and refine final outputs,
supporting teacher-in-the-loop practice, in contrast to unauditable automated
pipelines.
\end{abstract}
