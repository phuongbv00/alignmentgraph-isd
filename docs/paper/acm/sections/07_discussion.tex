\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The two experiments support claims at two different levels, and keeping them apart
is what makes either usable. TN1 is a system-level comparison: it can say how
AlignmentGraph-ISD stands against the benchmark agents at each size and whether that
standing changes as the model shrinks, but it cannot say which part of the design is
responsible. TN2 is where attribution happens, and only between the two factors it
manipulates. A statement that mixes the two---reading an ablation gradient as though
it explained the ladder gap---would not be supported by either. The findings from
each are \TBD.

Two readings will have to be distinguished rather than assumed. If the LLM judge
scores are close across the ablation arms while the alignment panel separates them,
the favourable reading is that general-purpose LLM judges are insensitive to alignment,
which is exactly what an independent alignment measurement exists to expose, and
known evaluator biases make that credible~\cite{panickssery2024llm}. The adversarial
reading is that the only instrument separating the arms shares a construct with the
design being ablated, while the mechanically independent LLM judge family does not---the
pattern one expects under weak discriminant validity. Automated signals alone cannot
choose between these, which is why the prediction that distinguishes them is
registered before the expert review is read (\Cref{sec:expert}) and why that review
is part of the design rather than future work.

The practical proposition is bounded in the same way. Explicit alignment state is
not free: the system spends more model calls and more tokens than a single-loop
agent, and the multiple is \TBD{} (\Cref{sec:cost}). The honest statement of that
trade is that the method exchanges additional cost for explicit state, an audit
trail, and controlled cross-artifact references---not that it is cheaper, which it
is not, at any size measured here. Whether the exchange is worth making is a
deployment question with a different answer at each end of the ladder, and the
evidence for each end is \TBD.

One design decision deserves to be stated as a finding rather than a footnote. An
earlier version of the harness closed missing relations with generated stand-ins---a
placeholder activity for an objective nothing practiced, a module for an
unsupported activity. Each of those satisfied its own check by existing, so coverage
looked complete while the design was not. Removing them raises the residual
violation counts this paper reports. That is the correct direction: a system whose
purpose is to make alignment checkable must not manufacture the relations it is
checking, and the boundary it draws---the harness may rearrange what the design
already contains, but only an agent may write instructional content---is what gives
the exported graph its evidentiary value. A tool that repairs its own audit is not
an audit.

\subsection{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
\label{sec:threats-limitations}

\paragraph{Internal validity.}
The 8192-token cap interacts with architecture: a decomposed system can finish
within a budget that truncates one-shot generation. This interaction is part of the
deployment condition rather than a confound to apologise for, so the cap is derived
from the checkable envelope in \Cref{sec:assumptions}, applied uniformly to every
system, and failures and truncations are reported per system and size
(\Cref{sec:robustness}). A higher-cap run remains a separate sensitivity, not a
change to the condition. The only baseline-specific intervention was translating
ReAct-ISD's prompt scaffolding into English; disclosing it permits an alternative
translation while holding the method fixed. LLM judge drift and self-preference remain
possible~\cite{panickssery2024llm}, mitigated by the no-LLM second instrument,
separate agent and LLM judge families, and the second LLM judge subset.

\paragraph{External validity.}
Conclusions are bounded by one model family. The four Qwen checkpoints share a
pretraining recipe, so capacity is confounded with recipe differences inside that
family, and results are stated on the Qwen3.5 0.8B--9B ladder only; extending them to
Llama, Gemma, or Phi is a hypothesis requiring another experiment. The scenarios come
from one stratified benchmark rather than institutional practice; stratification by
domain and difficulty mitigates narrow sampling, but field replication with
educator-authored briefs is still needed.

\paragraph{Construct validity.}
Similarity is not alignment. Family A measures textual correspondence, and its bridge
to constructive alignment rests on the measurement's evidence structure and on
established criteria~\cite{biggs2011teaching,porter2002measuring,webb1997criteria},
not on what the signals are named. The two instrument families are also not fully
independent: $\beta_{\mathrm{cong}}$ pairs an objective with an assessment task by
cosine before comparing Bloom levels, so it shares the embedding step with Family A,
and only $\beta_{\mathrm{port}}$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{webb}}$ are free of similarity
entirely. Triangulation claims therefore rest on those two. The primary Bloom
checkpoint is learned from one institution's coding
convention~\cite{li2022bloomclassification}; its individual decisions are not
traceable to Anderson--Krathwohl~\cite{anderson2001taxonomy}, and it has not been
validated on machine-generated objectives. Encoder and classifier error may be
differential across systems that paraphrase, repeat, or distribute content
differently, which could change a ranking rather than merely add noise; the
sensitivity star---independent neural lineages, a non-semantic floor, and a
classifier substitution---reduces this threat without removing it
(\Cref{sec:sensitivity}). Pinned revisions and cached embeddings reproduce the
reported signals up to floating-point noise, not bit for bit. Finally, the panel
measures properties of a document, not learning: it says nothing about what students
would learn from these designs.

\paragraph{Conclusion validity.}
Inference rests on 90 paired scenarios per size, with one deterministic generation
per cell. Uncertainty is therefore estimated over scenarios rather than over repeated
runs, which is the dominant source of variation at this sample size but leaves
run-to-run variability unestimated for most cells; the targeted repeats
(\Cref{sec:setup}) cover only the decisive cells and are reported separately with
their own $n$. Bootstrap intervals and exact permutation nulls for small paired
samples mitigate the small-sample limits; within-signal and panel-wide Holm
corrections expose the multiplicity trade-off. Output failure is a post-treatment
variable, so complete-case analysis can discard a system's worst cases:
\texttt{failure=0} is the primary layer, complete-case is a sensitivity computed over
available outputs, and failure rates are reported separately (\Cref{sec:stats}). The
failure policy, the panel rule, the trajectory-cap policy and the ablation prediction
were all fixed before any TN1 or TN2 output was scored, which limits
outcome-contingent interpretation without amounting to external preregistration.

\paragraph{Future directions.}
\label{sec:future-work}
Each direction follows from one of the limits above. A higher-cap sensitivity on a
larger device would test whether robustness and rankings persist when one-shot agents
receive a bigger serving allowance, with prompts, scenarios, decoding, failure policy
and reporting held fixed. A second model family would separate capacity from
pretraining recipe. The expert layer in this study is deliberately small; a larger
blind-coding study reporting Cohen's $\kappa$ against the full panel is what would
turn corroboration into construct validation.

A further limit is the generation order itself. Each artifact family is produced
once, in fixed ADDIE sequence, so checking can only repair inside the stage that has
just run, and a problem visible only after a later family exists cannot send the
system back. Feasibility is the clearest case: total learner workload and resource
demand are properties of the finished design that no stage-local check can act on. A
state-driven variant would select the next stage from the current graph and treat
termination as a conjunctive property of that graph---structural completeness,
constraint satisfaction, and a stated alignment condition---rather than as a
completed visit list. Orchestrators that re-plan from shared state are established
outside education~\cite{fourney2024magenticone,zhang2026patchboard}, and
pedagogically driven course generation was posed as non-linear planning before LLMs
~\cite{ullrich2008paigos}, while LLM-based instructional-design systems remain
fixed-role pipelines~\cite{zhang2025eduplanner,yao2025instructionalagents,
isdagentbench}. Two conditions would make such a study interpretable. The
fixed-order system reported here is the required baseline, because the contrast is
orchestration policy rather than agent count. And the stopping condition must not be
the alignment panel: driving generation with the same signal that evaluates it would
separate that signal from the construct it stands for~\cite{gao2023scaling}, so the
loop would have to stop on structural and constraint predicates while the panel
remains an untouched post-hoc measurement.

Finally, because the panel measures textual correspondence and cognitive demand
rather than learning outcomes, connecting its profiles to educator decisions and to
what learners actually achieve is the step that would license any claim about
instructional effectiveness.
