\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The ladder and ablation support different claims. At the system level, the ADDIE
advantage is largest at the small end of the Qwen 0.8B--9B ladder and contracts to
parity with ReAct-ISD at 9B; the RQ2 profile satisfies its pre-specified rule at
every size (\Cref{sec:rq1,sec:rq2}). At the mechanism level, removing graph context
shifts the alignment panel downward while ADDIE remains nearly invariant
(\Cref{sec:ablation}). Explicit shared state is therefore supported as the mechanism
carrying the measured alignment profile, but not as an improvement to judge-scored
quality or as a mechanism whose effect strengthens toward smaller models. The
verifier has no score effect in the shipped configuration; it is a backstop and an
audit trail.

Every ablation arm produced usable output, which locates robustness in the execution
structure common to those arms rather than in graph context or verification alone.
Because no monolithic arm exists, this remains a system-level attribution, not a
causal estimate for decomposition. More broadly, the study complements evidence
that harness sensitivity can be non-monotonic across model tiers
~\cite{cho2026harness}: the observed interaction, rather than prior literature,
supports the small-end result. Numerical comparison with studies using different
models, tasks, budgets, or judges would not support the same inference.

The practical proposition is that the harness runs reliably and remains worth
running at the small end within the serving envelope in \Cref{sec:assumptions}.
Its case weakens as capacity rises: at the smallest size it loses to one baseline on
\texttt{total\_score}, while at 9B ReAct-ISD is equal or better depending on the
judge signal and uses substantially fewer tokens (\Cref{sec:cost}).
AlignmentGraph-ISD is therefore decision support, not expert replacement. Its graph dump gives an
educator an audit-and-override interface for tracing relations and verifier actions;
professional review remains necessary for pedagogical suitability, context,
accessibility, and use.

The judge-invariant but alignment-shifted ablation admits two competing readings.
On the favorable reading, general LLM judges are insensitive to alignment, which is
the measurement gap addressed by the independent RQ2 protocol; known evaluator
biases make that possibility credible~\cite{panickssery2024llm}. On the adversarial
reading, only the instrument designed around the ablated construct separates the
arms, while the mechanically independent judge family does not---the pattern
expected under weak discriminant validity. The current evidence cannot choose
between them. This ambiguity motivates the expert-validation direction specified
below.

These interpretations are bounded by the study design and the instruments that
produce them. The following threats state those boundaries together with the
corresponding mitigations, then derive the next experiments from what remains
unresolved.

\subsection{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
\label{sec:threats-limitations}

Internal validity is affected by the interaction between the 8192-token cap and
architecture: decomposition can complete within a budget that truncates one-shot
generation. This interaction is also part of the deployment condition, so we derive
the cap from the checkable envelope in \Cref{sec:assumptions}, apply it uniformly,
and report failures and truncation by agent and size (\Cref{sec:robustness}). A
higher-cap run remains a separate sensitivity rather than a change to that condition.
The only baseline-specific intervention was translating ReAct-ISD's prompt
scaffolding into English; disclosure permits alternative translations while holding
the method fixed. LLM-judge drift and self-preference remain possible
~\cite{panickssery2024llm}, mitigated by the no-LLM second instrument, separate
agent and judge families, and same-session ablation contrasts.

External validity is bounded by one model family. The four Qwen checkpoints share a
pretraining recipe, so size is confounded with recipe differences within that
family. Conclusions therefore apply only across the Qwen 0.8B--9B ladder;
generalization to Llama, Gemma, or Phi requires another experiment. The scenarios
also come from one stratified benchmark rather than institutional practice. Domain
and difficulty stratification mitigate narrow sampling, but educator-reviewed field
replication is still needed.

Construct validity is the central limitation: similarity is not alignment. Family A
measures textual correspondence, and its bridge to constructive alignment rests on
the protocol's evidence structure and established criteria
~\cite{biggs2011teaching,porter2002measuring,webb1997criteria}, not its metric names.
In particular, \texttt{assessment\_objective\_similarity} cannot distinguish useful
coverage from restatement and is excluded from directional counts. The primary
Bloom checkpoint is learned from one institution's coding convention
~\cite{li2022bloomclassification}; its individual decisions are not traceable to
Anderson--Krathwohl~\cite{anderson2001taxonomy}, nor has it been validated on
machine-generated objectives. Encoder and classifier error may be differential
across systems that paraphrase, repeat, or distribute content differently. The
three-axis sensitivity star---independent neural lineages, a TF--IDF floor, and a
BERT--lexicon substitution---reduces but does not remove this threat
(\Cref{sec:sensitivity}). Pinned revisions and cached embeddings reproduce reported
scores up to neural floating-point noise, not necessarily bit for bit.

Conclusion validity depends on \ladderNumScenarios{} paired scenarios and
\ladderNumRunsPerModel{} runs per cell. The run count limits variance estimation;
between-run SDs, bootstrap intervals, and exact permutation nulls for small paired
samples mitigate that limit. Within-signal and panel-wide Holm corrections expose
the multiplicity trade-off. Output failure is post-treatment, so complete-case
analysis can discard an agent's worst cases; \texttt{failure=0} is primary,
complete-case is sensitivity rather than unconditional, and failure rates are
reported separately (\Cref{sec:stats}). The failure policy and panel rule were fixed
before pooled RQ2 results were inspected, limiting outcome-contingent interpretation
without claiming external preregistration.

\paragraph{Future directions.}
\label{sec:future-work}
Each next experiment follows from one of these limits. Because every ablation
retains the specialist workflow, a monolithic arm in the same package and under the
same per-scenario token budget should separate decomposition from codebase,
prompting, and test-time compute. A higher-cap sensitivity on the rented 24\,GB
device should test whether robustness and rankings persist when one-shot agents
receive a larger serving allowance while prompts, scenarios, decoding, failure
policy, and reporting rules remain fixed.

The missing human evidence tier motivates a study in which two ISD experts
blind-code a stratified sample, report inter-expert agreement, and test
machine--human agreement with Cohen's $\kappa$ under a prediction fixed before
labels are examined. That study would also adjudicate the competing ablation
readings above. Finally, because the panel measures textual correspondence and
cognitive-demand signals rather than learning outcomes, subsequent work must
connect its profiles to educator decisions and learner outcomes before making
claims about instructional effectiveness.
