\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:experimental-methodology}

\subsection{Research Questions}
\label{sec:rqs}

RQ1 separates quality level from scale interaction so that per-size significance is
not mistaken for evidence about downscaling.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1a (quality level).} At each size in the Qwen 0.8B--9B ladder,
  how does AlignmentGraph-ISD compare with the six benchmark agents?
  \item \textbf{RQ1b (scale interaction).} Does its quality gap relative to those
  agents widen as the model shrinks?
  \item \textbf{RQ2 (constructive-alignment signals).} How do measurable signals
  of constructive alignment vary with model size, and does the comparative gap
  widen as the model shrinks?
\end{itemize}
RQ1a and RQ1b are co-primary. RQ2 intentionally stops at measurable signals;
construct validation requires expert evidence beyond automated scores.

\subsection{Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}

We use the \texttt{test\_90} split of ISD-Agent-Bench~\cite{isdagentbench}: 90
scenarios selected by stratified sampling with seed 42. Every condition receives
the same scenarios, and the benchmark supplies the six comparison agents.

\subsection{Models and Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

The ladder comprises Qwen checkpoints at 0.8B, 2B, 4B, and 9B, holding model family
constant while capacity changes. Each agent--size cell has three independent runs
over all 90 scenarios. Two fixed judges from families separate from the agents,
\texttt{openai/gpt-4o-mini} and \texttt{google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite}, score every
artifact; following the benchmark, the scenario score is their mean. This separation
reduces self-preference risk~\cite{panickssery2024llm}.

All agents run through vLLM with streaming enabled, thinking disabled, and a common
generation cap. We cap generation at 8192 tokens per call --- the budget a 4B model leaves on a single 12--16 GB GPU, the deployment target this work claims. The 24--32 GB pods were rental convenience for running the ladder in parallel, not the claimed envelope.
Whether an architecture completes within that cap is therefore a measured deployment
property. Scenarios and prompts for all seven ladder agents are English by design.
The only baseline-specific intervention translates ReAct-ISD's prompt scaffolding
into English without changing its method. Manifests record checkpoints, prompts,
seeds, decoding settings, and judges.

\subsection{Conditions}
\label{sec:conditions}

The primary comparison includes AlignmentGraph-ISD, Baseline, EduPlanner,
ADDIE-Agent, RPISD-Agent, Dick-Carey-Agent, and ReAct-ISD at every size and run.
Three proposal ablations run inside the same ladder sessions: $-$Verifier retains
graph context and specialist decomposition; $-$GraphContext retains the verifier
and decomposition; and the skeleton retains only decomposition. Their arm-minus-A0
deltas therefore share a judge session and avoid between-session judge drift. The
in-session A0 also provides a consistency check against the three primary A0 runs.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

RQ1 reports \texttt{addie\_median}, \texttt{trajectory\_score}, and
\texttt{total\_score} in parallel with identical analyses and equal standing.
ADDIE leads the narrative because its artifact rubric matches RQ1, whereas
Trajectory measures process conformance. The benchmark composite is retained for
comparability and applies its fixed $0.7\,\text{ADDIE}+0.3\,\text{trajectory}$
weighting. We average the judges within scenario and run, then use the
scenario-level mean across runs for inference.

RQ2 applies the protocol in \Cref{sec:protocol} offline to saved outputs. Its
primary encoder is \texttt{nvidia/llama-embed-nemotron-8b}
~\cite{babakhin2025nemotron}; independent-backbone replications use
\texttt{BAAI/bge-m3}~\cite{chen2024m3embedding} and
\texttt{ibm-granite/granite-embedding-311m-multilingual-r2}
~\cite{awasthy2026granite}. A character-$n$-gram TF--IDF encoder supplies a lexical
floor. The primary Bloom instrument is a six-class \texttt{bert-base-uncased}
checkpoint fine-tuned on the single-label EDM2022 learning-objective corpus
~\cite{anderson2001taxonomy,li2022bloomclassification}; its sensitivity arm is an
Anderson--Krathwohl verb lexicon. The checkpoint always returns an argmax, whereas
the lexicon can abstain, so their Family-B denominators may differ.

Sensitivity uses a star around Nemotron+BERT, not a grid: BGE-M3, Granite, and
TF--IDF vary the encoder one at a time, while the lexicon varies only the Bloom
instrument. These arms test three questions---neural-encoder lineage, dependence on
semantic embeddings, and Bloom-instrument choice. The pre-specified panel rule
declares an advantage at a size when at least four of six directional signals are
significantly positive after within-signal Holm correction and none is significantly
negative; the reversed pattern indicates disadvantage, and all others are mixed.
The seventh, non-directional signal is still reported and tested.

The primary failure layer assigns zero to a missing output. Complete-case results
are sensitivity analyses, never unconditional; RQ1 also reports
\texttt{failure=min}, while RQ2 does not because its signals already have floor
zero. Failure rates are analyzed separately. For cost telemetry, only
\texttt{metadata.token\_usage} is read. \emph{Operational reproducibility:} the scoring procedure uses no LLM and has no stochastic component; results are reproducible given pinned encoder versions and the embedding cache.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
\label{sec:stats}

The unit of inference is a scenario's mean over three runs. For RQ1a, paired
Wilcoxon signed-rank tests compare A0 with each baseline at every size and for all
three judge signals; RQ2 applies the same test to each panel signal. When the paired
sample has $n\leq25$, we use the exact permutation null. We report paired mean
differences, rank-biserial effects, and bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals from
10{,}000 scenario resamples with seed 42.

Holm correction covers the six proposed-versus-baseline comparisons per size,
separately for each signal. RQ2 additionally reports panel-wide Holm correction
over seven signals and six comparisons as multiplicity sensitivity; this correction
does not create a signal hierarchy. For RQ1b, let $\Delta_s$ be the paired
A0-minus-baseline gap at size $s$. The pre-specified interaction is
$\Delta_{\mathrm{largest}}-\Delta_{\mathrm{smallest}}$, tested with paired Wilcoxon
and a bootstrap interval; a negative value means a larger A0 advantage at the small
end. RQ2 uses the same interaction, with
\texttt{objective\_assessment\_similarity} leading the scale narrative and the
other signals reported alongside it.

Missing outputs make complete-case inference condition on a post-treatment
variable and preferentially remove severe failures, especially at smaller sizes.
This motivates \texttt{failure=0} as the primary layer and complete-case as
sensitivity. Fisher's exact tests with Holm correction compare output-failure
rates.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:reproducibility}

All measured values are generated from saved run, judge, alignment, and ablation
artifacts and enter the paper only through files in \texttt{docs/generated/}.
Every table cell pairs its mean with the between-run SD of that signal. Manifests pin
runtime and model settings; revision-aware embedding caches support exact
re-analysis from saved vectors. Results produced before the study pivot are excluded.
