\section{AlignmentGraph-ISD}
\label{sec:method}

AlignmentGraph-ISD generates a complete ADDIE program from a design brief. Its
organizing principle is a division of labour that the rest of this section makes
precise: the model writes instructional content and nothing else, while a
deterministic harness decides what is asked for, in what order, under what
contract, what is committed, what is checked, and what is reported. Every claim
this paper makes about auditability rests on that line, because only the harness
side leaves a record that can be replayed. \Cref{fig:architecture} draws the
result and \Cref{alg:harness} states it as a procedure.

\subsection{Role decomposition by ISD concern}
\label{sec:decomposition}

A complete ADDIE program is a long-form, multiply constrained generation task; a
single-shot system requires the model to hold the entire evolving program inside
one call. For small models, several lines of evidence indicate that decomposition
is a principled alternative: prompt decomposition helps on tasks that exceed the
capacity of a single call~\cite{zhou2023leasttomost,khot2023decomposed};
separating generation from checking improves constraint compliance
~\cite{ferraz2024decrim}; small models are weak at tool use, but decomposed
multi-agent frameworks narrow the gap with large models
~\cite{shen2024multillmagent}; agent scaffolding carries much of the quality at
0.5B--3B scale~\cite{kang2025agentdistill}; and SLMs may suffice for agentic
workloads when the architecture handles coordination~\cite{belcak2025slm}. In ISD,
decomposition also matches the domain: the process already separates
analysis, outcome, assessment, activity, content, and implementation concerns
~\cite{branch2009addie}. AlignmentGraph-ISD therefore runs six stages, one per
concern, each under its own agent identity (\Cref{tab:specialists}):
\[
\mathcal{S}=\{s_{\mathrm{ana}},\,s_{\mathrm{out}},\,s_{\mathrm{asm}},\,
s_{\mathrm{act}},\,s_{\mathrm{con}},\,s_{\mathrm{imp}}\}.
\]
The stage \emph{order} is instructed and fixed; the agents are autonomous in how a
stage is carried out, never in which stage runs next. The system is therefore a
workflow-style, role-decomposed multi-agent system in the SOP family
~\cite{hong2024metagpt}: coordination is static, and there is no negotiation and no
dynamic planning between roles.

This decomposition is not assumed to be beneficial. It is one of the two factors
of the ablation in \Cref{sec:ablation}, which compares it against a single agent
carrying one context through all six stages.

Decomposition solves one problem and creates another: it splits the program into
artifacts written by different calls, so no single call holds the consistency among
them---the property at the centre of constructive alignment. The stages therefore
need shared state.

\subsection{The alignment graph}
\label{sec:alignment-graph}

The form of that shared state follows from an educational-theory premise.
Constructive alignment locates the quality of an instructional design in the
relationships among learning objectives, teaching--learning activities, and
assessment, rather than in the quality of each artifact in isolation
~\cite{biggs1996enhancing,biggs2011teaching}. Quality-review practice operates on
the same relationships: Quality Matters requires measurable objectives and requires
assessments, materials, and activities to support them~\cite{qm2018rubric}. If
quality resides in relationships, those relationships belong in generation state as
first-class objects rather than being inferred from prose afterwards.
AlignmentGraph-ISD implements this through a shared graph $G=(V,E)$ whose nodes are
design artifacts and whose edges are typed relations (\Cref{tab:graph-spec}).

\begin{table}[t]
  \caption{Components of the alignment graph $G=(V,E)$.}
  \label{tab:graph-spec}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lp{0.6\columnwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    Symbol & Specification \\
    \midrule
    $v\in V$ & Node: a design artifact with type $\tau(v)\in\mathcal{T}$, where
    $\mathcal{T}$ is exactly the artifact kinds owned by the stages in
    \Cref{tab:specialists}. \\
    $\mathcal{R}$ & Sixteen relation types in two tiers. Five \emph{core}
    relations $C_1$--$C_5$ each carry a theoretical warrant from constructive
    alignment or backward design and form the construct-validity backbone; eleven
    \emph{secondary} relations $P_1$--$P_{11}$ record provenance and delivery
    traceability and are never offered as construct-validity evidence. \\
    $(v_s,v_d,r)\in E$ & Edge: $v_s$ relates to $v_d$ through $r\in\mathcal{R}$.
    Each edge records its evidence, its rationale, and the mechanism that
    established it (a resolved identifier, a type match, or provenance). \\
    $E_r$ & The pairs joined by $r$: $E_r=\{(v_s,v_d)\colon(v_s,v_d,r)\in E\}$,
    used in \Cref{eq:aligned}. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Three core relations are the edges of the constructive-alignment triad: $C_2$
(\emph{assessed\_by}) joins an objective to an assessment task, $C_3$
(\emph{practiced\_by}) joins an objective to a learning activity, and $C_4$
(\emph{prepares\_for}) joins an activity to the assessment task it prepares
learners for. An objective has structural alignment when those three close on it:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:aligned}
\mathrm{aligned}(o) \iff \exists\,t,a\colon
(o,t)\in E_{C_3}\;\wedge\;(o,a)\in E_{C_2}\;\wedge\;(t,a)\in E_{C_4},
\end{equation}
that is, $o$ is practiced by an activity $t$ that prepares for an assessment task
$a$ that measures $o$. The quantifier matters. Taken as three independent
existentials, the predicate is satisfied by an objective whose activity prepares
for some unrelated task, which is two spokes rather than a triangle;
\Cref{eq:aligned} requires the triangle to meet. The remaining two core relations
place the surrounding artifacts: $C_1$ decomposes a course goal into objectives and
$C_5$ joins a content module to the activity it supports.

\Cref{eq:aligned} is deliberately structural. Whether an assessment task is pitched
at the cognitive level its objective declares is a property of the content inside
the nodes, not of the edges between them; it is checked separately
(\Cref{sec:qc}) and reported on its own, so that a coverage number never silently
mixes a missing relation with a mismatched verb.

Every stage $s$ is bound to the graph through a triple
\begin{gather*}
s=\langle C(s),\; g_s,\; W(s)\rangle,\\
C(s)\subseteq G,\qquad
W(s)=(\mathcal{T}_s,\mathcal{R}_s),\quad
\mathcal{T}_s\subseteq\mathcal{T},\;
\mathcal{R}_s\subseteq\mathcal{R},
\end{gather*}
comprising a read scope, a generation step, and a write contract:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Read}---scope $C(s)$: the stage may read only the artifact kinds its
  concern depends on. Upstream premises therefore enter the call as structured
  artifacts rather than being reconstructed from prose.
  \item \emph{Generate}---$g_s$: the model writes content for this concern, and
  each item declares the identifier of the upstream artifact it serves.
  \item \emph{Write}---contract $W(s)$: the stage may create only node types in
  $\mathcal{T}_s$ and relations in $\mathcal{R}_s$. The harness resolves each
  declared identifier by direct lookup and writes the typed edge itself. Relations
  therefore originate at commit time, and no stage can silently alter an upstream
  premise.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stage execution}
\label{sec:stage-loop}

Each stage is an act--observe loop in the sense of ReAct~\cite{yao2023react},
carried out through native tool calls rather than parsed from free text. The
harness binds a stage-specific tool set: read tools that list the graph or return
one artifact in full, and one \emph{submit} tool per artifact the stage owns. There
is no separate generation step---the artifact \emph{is} the arguments of the submit
call. This is what allows the harness to hold the contract without asking the model
to respect a format: a submit whose arguments fail the artifact's validator is
rejected, and the rejection carries the validator's own error messages back into
the conversation as the next observation, so the model sees exactly what it must
change. A stage ends when every artifact it owns has been committed, or when its
turn budget is exhausted, in which case the best material the model produced is
committed and the stage is recorded as having given up---a number this paper
reports rather than concealing behind a fallback.

Binding one submit tool per artifact, rather than one per stage, matters at small
scale for a concrete reason: with a per-call completion cap, a stage that must emit
all of its artifacts in a single call fails entirely when the cap is reached,
whereas a rejected artifact costs only that artifact.

\subsection{Designers plan, developers write}
\label{sec:fanout}

Three stages own artifact families whose size grows with the design: assessment
tasks, learning activities, learner materials, and session guides. For these, the
stage agent does not write the items. It submits a \emph{development plan} that
divides the family into work sets---which objectives, which instrument, which
theme---and the harness dispatches each set to a developer agent that writes only
that set. The division is the design decision the stage is asked to make; the
writing is delegated.

Two properties of this tier are the harness's, not the model's. Identifiers are
reserved by composition: a work set receives an ordinal when the plan is numbered,
and each artifact within it is numbered from one, so an identifier is fixed before
any developer runs and no two developers can collide. References within a family
are written by position and resolved to the assigned identifiers afterwards, with
unresolvable references dropped rather than committed---a dangling identifier in
the graph is invisible, whereas a dropped reference shows up in the coverage
numbers. Where a plan leaves a family incomplete, the harness returns the gap to
the stage agent, which answers with another plan rather than with artifacts.

\subsection{Checking and repair}
\label{sec:qc}

The graph guarantees the structural layer: every relation is explicit,
\Cref{eq:aligned} is machine-checkable, and a missing edge is visible. The content
inside the nodes is the product of $g_s$ and can fail in ways an edge cannot
express---an assessment task can exist, be correctly linked, and still not elicit
the cognitive level its objective declares. After each commit, the harness runs the
deterministic checks that the committing stage owns (\Cref{tab:checks});
ownership follows $W(s)$, so no stage is asked about something it cannot write.

Repair has two tiers, and the boundary between them is a claim this work takes
seriously. The first tier is deterministic and never calls a model: it
\emph{rearranges} what the design already contains---re-pointing a dangling
reference to the artifact that exists, replacing an out-of-taxonomy verb with the
one the declared level licenses, reformatting a choice item that carries no answer
key. The second tier is the owning agent itself: a violation whose fix requires
writing is handed back to that agent as a repair turn inside its own conversation,
so the request, the answer, and the resulting artifact are three consecutive turns
of one transcript.

What the harness will \emph{not} do is write instructional content. A missing
activity, a rubric criterion that does not describe its item, a delivery decision
that does not fit the brief---each of these was at one point closed by a template,
and each template satisfied its own check by existing. Those templates were removed;
the corresponding violation types are shown to their owning agent, counted when they
remain, and never filled in by the harness. Residual violation counts are higher as
a result. That is the number becoming honest, and it is what makes the audit dump
worth reading.

\subsection{Orchestration and audit}
\label{sec:orchestration}

\Cref{alg:harness} states the whole procedure. The stages run in one fixed order,
so a stage sees every upstream artifact through $C(s)$ and cannot alter it: the
sequence, not a routing decision, determines what is available when. Checking and
repair are local to the stage that has just committed. After the last stage, a
single reconciliation pass addresses violations that span stages, assigning each to
the owner recorded for its type; this pass is deterministic and takes no model call.
It runs once, after the sequence, rather than being re-entered between stages,
because an unbounded check--repair--recheck cycle on a small model does not
terminate reliably.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{AlignmentGraph-ISD execution}
  \label{alg:harness}
  \KwIn{design brief $b$; turn budget $T$; reconciliation budget $K$}
  \KwOut{ADDIE program; graph dump (JSON + DOT)}
  $G \leftarrow \emptyset$\;
  \For{$s \in (s_{\mathrm{ana}}, s_{\mathrm{out}}, s_{\mathrm{asm}},
       s_{\mathrm{act}}, s_{\mathrm{con}}, s_{\mathrm{imp}})$}{
    bind tools: read over $C(s)$, one submit per artifact in $W(s)$\;
    \While{$s$ has artifacts left \textbf{and} turns used $< T$}{
      $call \leftarrow g_s(\text{transcript})$ \tcp*{model acts}
      \uIf{$call$ is a read}{append its result to the transcript\;}
      \uElseIf{$\mathrm{validate}(call) = \emptyset$}{
        $G \leftarrow \mathrm{commit}(G,\, call,\, W(s))$\;
        $V \leftarrow \mathrm{check}(G,\, s)$\;
        $G \leftarrow \mathrm{repair}_{\mathrm{struct}}(G,\, V)$
          \tcp*{model-free}
        hand the remainder of $V$ back to $s$ as a repair turn\;
      }
      \Else{return the validator's errors as the observation\;}
    }
    \ForEach{work set in $s$'s development plan}{
      dispatch to a developer agent; commit its set under $W(s)$\;
    }
  }
  $V \leftarrow \mathrm{check}(G)$ \tcp*{whole graph}
  route each $v \in V$ to its owner and repair structurally, $\le K$ rounds\;
  $output \leftarrow \mathrm{aggregate}(G)$\;
  $G \leftarrow \mathrm{audit}(G)$ \tcp*{residual $\to$ warnings}
  \Return $(output,\ \mathrm{dump}(G))$\;
\end{algorithm}

At the end of every run the complete graph is exported as JSON and DOT, separately
from the execution trajectory. An instructional designer can trace which task
measures which objective, see which objectives fail \Cref{eq:aligned} and on which
leg, read the violations that were found and what became of each, and then revise
or override the design---the teacher-in-the-loop principle made operational rather
than asserted. The ADDIE program is assembled from the same graph, so the document
a reader receives and the record they audit cannot disagree.

The two design choices carry different kinds of evidence in this study, and the
ablation in \Cref{sec:ablation} separates them: how the work is decomposed across
agents, and what substrate those agents share. Both are tested; neither is assumed.
