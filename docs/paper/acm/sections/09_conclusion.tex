\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Constructive alignment becomes difficult to inspect when objectives, activities,
assessments, and evaluation evidence are generated as one fluent document. Under a
small-model serving envelope, the harness must also preserve those relations without
placing the whole task in one call.

This paper contributes two responses: AlignmentGraph-ISD, which coordinates
specialists through explicit graph state and exports that state for audit; and a
no-LLM evaluation protocol that reports a flat panel of textual-correspondence and
cognitive-demand signals. The protocol measures signals of constructive alignment,
not the validated construct itself.

Across the Qwen 0.8B--9B ladder, the harness produced usable output for every
scenario, led ADDIE and the pre-specified RQ2 profile at every size, and showed its
largest relative advantage toward the small end. The three judge signals remain
co-equal: \texttt{total\_score} loses to one baseline at the smallest size, and
ReAct-ISD reaches ADDIE parity at 9B.

The ablation narrows the mechanism claim. Removing graph context lowers the RQ2
panel at every size while leaving ADDIE nearly invariant; the verifier is a repair
backstop and source of audit evidence, not a supported score effect in the shipped
configuration. Because every arm retains specialist decomposition, its contribution
to robustness is established only at the system level.

The evidence supports educator-in-the-loop use of an auditable graph-guided harness
within the evaluated ladder. A blind expert study is the next step for testing
machine--human agreement and distinguishing the favorable and adversarial readings
of the ablation.
