\begin{abstract}
LLM-based agents can generate complete instructional programs, but their quality
depends on \emph{constructive alignment}---the correspondence among learning
objectives, activities, and assessments---which is the first property to break
down as organizations move from large models to cost-efficient small language
models (SLMs) for internal training-content generation. Alignment degradation is
a cumulative, invisible defect: a program still looks reasonable section by
section and only fails at deployment. Existing LLM-agent approaches for
instructional systems design (ISD) merely \emph{encourage} alignment through
prompting; none represents it as state the system can check and repair, so
quality is left to the raw capability of the model. We propose
\textbf{AlignmentGraph-ISD}, a graph-guided multi-agent harness that reifies
constructive alignment as an explicit, verifiable \emph{alignment graph} and
organizes generation around it: specialist agents build the graph under a fixed instructional-design workflow
with bounded write scope, so alignment forms by construction, and each specialist
runs pedagogical verification and repair as a tool within its own turn. We
evaluate the harness on ISD-Agent-Bench against six representative agents under
both a large model and a 9B SLM, and conduct an ablation that isolates the
contribution of each mechanism. On the benchmark, AlignmentGraph-ISD achieves the
highest overall score among the seven agents; the ablation shows that multi-agent
decomposition over the graph is the dominant contributor, and---crucially---its
contribution roughly doubles as the model shrinks (from $+3.9$ composite points on
the LLM to $+7.9$ on the SLM, $p < 10^{-12}$), while the harness's own score stays
nearly invariant across scale as the baselines degrade. Pedagogical
verification/repair leaves the judged score unchanged on both models even as it
measurably reduces residual alignment defects. These findings show that
representing alignment as first-class graph state, and decomposing generation
around it, is what lets an agent harness preserve instructional-design quality
independently of the underlying model.
\end{abstract}
